Strategy

What this strategy is, and what would kill it

What it does. Instead of paying the spread to get in — which costs a median 33% of the entry price for a round trip, and is why every earlier engine lost — this one charges the spread. It posts a buy and a sell at the same time and takes no view on who wins. If both fill, it pockets the gap and holds nothing.

Why only wide spreads. Sometimes only one side fills and the price keeps going, leaving inventory that settles at zero. That cost is roughly fixed, so the spread has to be big enough to cover it: at 1 tick we capture 0.13¢/share and lose badly; at 6+ ticks we capture 3.4–7.2¢ and the backtest turns positive. Below the threshold it quotes nothing at all, so being idle is normal.

What the backtest said. +$3,319/week pooled over six sports at a realistic 0.2s latency, 95% CI [+$1,717, +$5,082], 21 of 29 sport-days positive, still +$2,395 after dropping the two best days. It replicated in five sports beyond the tennis it was tuned on, and a separate implementation of the live engine reproduced tennis to within 0.5%.

The thing that could kill it — read this one. A 6-tick spread survives a median 1.1 seconds before someone improves the bid or the ask. That someone is a competitor doing exactly what we do. Losing that race by a single tick takes tennis from +$2,027 to +$227. The backtest cannot settle this, because it assumes every trade that historically crossed would have come to us — and our recorded tape only contains a world in which we were never quoting.

So the number that matters is not the P&L. It is beaten to price against solo quoting. Lots of solo time and few beats means these thin spreads really are unattended and this is worth real money. Beats dominating means it is a speed race we have no particular reason to win, and it should be switched off.

Two ways the figures flatter themselves. unrealised marks open positions at the last mid, but exiting one means crossing the spread, so treat net incl. open as an upper bound and settled net as the honest number. And settled is the share of closed markets valued at their real settlement rather than the last price seen; if it sits well below 100% the P&L is inflated. Marking at last-seen price once produced a fake +323% return at a 100% win rate in this project.

No real money is at risk. This engine has no path to the order broker; it records what would have happened.
